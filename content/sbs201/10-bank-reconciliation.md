# Week 10 — Bank Reconciliation

> Sample material written for the Curriculum AI demo. Not an official Strathmore University document.

## Why the balances differ

The business's cash book and the bank statement record the same account, but rarely show the same balance at month end. There are two kinds of difference:

1. **Items the bank knows about but the business has not yet recorded.** Examples: bank charges, interest, standing orders, direct credits (including M-Pesa Paybill receipts settled to the account), and dishonoured cheques. **These are corrected in the cash book.**
2. **Timing differences.** The business has recorded the item, but the bank has not processed it yet:
   - **Unpresented cheques:** cheques written by the business that the payee has not yet banked.
   - **Outstanding lodgements (deposits in transit):** money paid in that the bank has not yet credited.

   **These appear only in the reconciliation statement.**

## Method

1. Compare the cash book with the bank statement and tick matching items.
2. **Update the cash book** for unticked items that appear only on the statement.
3. Prepare the **bank reconciliation statement**: start with the balance per the bank statement, add outstanding lodgements and deduct unpresented cheques. The result must equal the updated cash book balance.

## Worked example

At 30 June, Bidii Traders' cash book shows a debit balance of **KES 84,500**. The bank statement shows **KES 102,700** in credit. Investigation finds:

- Bank charges of KES 1,200 are not in the cash book.
- A customer paid KES 15,000 through M-Pesa Paybill directly into the bank; not in the cash book.
- A customer's cheque for KES 6,000 was dishonoured; not in the cash book.
- Cheques of KES 32,400 issued in late June have not been presented.
- A lodgement of KES 22,000 on 30 June has not yet been credited by the bank.

### Step 1: Updated cash book

| | KES |
|---|---|
| Balance per cash book | 84,500 |
| Add: Direct credit (M-Pesa Paybill) | 15,000 |
| Less: Bank charges | (1,200) |
| Less: Dishonoured cheque | (6,000) |
| **Updated cash book balance** | **92,300** |

### Step 2: Bank reconciliation statement at 30 June

| | KES |
|---|---|
| Balance per bank statement | 102,700 |
| Add: Outstanding lodgement | 22,000 |
| Less: Unpresented cheques | (32,400) |
| **Balance per updated cash book** | **92,300** |

The two figures agree, so the reconciliation is complete. The **KES 92,300** is the figure shown in the statement of financial position.

## Watch the sign convention

A **debit** balance in the cash book means money in the bank. On the bank statement the same position appears as a **credit** balance, because to the bank your money is a liability (it owes it to you). An overdraft is a credit in the cash book and a debit on the statement.

## Common mistakes

1. Putting bank charges in the reconciliation statement instead of the cash book.
2. Adding unpresented cheques instead of deducting them.
3. Using the original cash book balance in the statement of financial position.
