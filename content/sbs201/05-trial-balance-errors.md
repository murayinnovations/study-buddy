# Week 5 — Trial Balance and Correction of Errors

> Sample material written for the Arthur demo. Not an official Strathmore University document.

## What a trial balance proves

A trial balance lists the balance on every ledger account. Debit balances go in one column, credit balances in the other, and the totals should agree.

Agreement only proves that **total debits equal total credits**. It does not prove the books are correct.

## Errors the trial balance does NOT reveal

Both sides of the entry are affected equally, so the totals still agree.

| Error | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Omission | Transaction not recorded at all | A sales invoice is lost and never entered |
| Commission | Right amount, wrong account of the **same type** | Payment from customer Otieno credited to customer Ochieng |
| Principle | Entry in the wrong **class** of account | Repairs to a vehicle (expense) debited to the Vehicles account (asset) |
| Original entry | Wrong amount used on both sides | Stationery of KES 4,500 recorded as KES 5,400 |
| Complete reversal | Debit and credit swapped | Cash sale debited to Sales and credited to Cash |
| Compensating | Two errors that cancel out | Sales overcast by 1,000 and wages overcast by 1,000 |

## Errors the trial balance DOES reveal

Only one side is wrong, or the amounts differ, so the totals disagree. Examples: a single-sided entry, a casting (addition) error in one account, or a balance copied into the wrong column.

## The suspense account

If the totals disagree and the error cannot be found straight away, the difference is put into a **suspense account** so the trial balance agrees. When each error is found, a journal entry corrects it and clears the suspense account.

## Worked examples

**1. Sales day book undercast by KES 2,000.**
The customers' accounts were posted correctly, but the Sales account was credited KES 2,000 too little. Debits exceed credits by 2,000, so a suspense account was opened with a **credit** balance of 2,000.

Correction: **Dr** Suspense 2,000, **Cr** Sales 2,000

**2. Stationery of KES 4,500 bought on credit was recorded as KES 5,400.**
Error of original entry. No suspense entry needed.

Correction: **Dr** Payables 900, **Cr** Stationery 900

**3. A payment of KES 8,000 to a supplier was debited to Purchases instead of the supplier's account.**
Error of principle (an expense was debited instead of a liability). No suspense entry needed.

Correction: **Dr** Payables 8,000, **Cr** Purchases 8,000

## Exam tips

1. For each error, first ask: "Did this make the trial balance disagree?" Only those errors go through suspense.
2. Write every correction as a journal entry with a short narrative.
3. If the question asks for the effect on profit, check whether the correction touches an income statement account.
