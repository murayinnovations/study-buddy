# Week 8 — Accruals and Prepayments

> Sample material written for the Curriculum AI demo. Not an official Strathmore University document.

## The accrual basis

Financial statements are prepared on the **accrual basis** (IAS 1): income and expenses are recorded in the period they relate to, **not** when cash is paid or received.

The most common misconception in this topic is "an expense is whatever cash went out this year". It is not. The expense is what was **used** this year.

## The four adjustments

| Situation | Name | Shown as | Effect on the year's figure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expense used, not yet paid | Accrued expense (accrual) | Current liability | Expense goes **up** |
| Expense paid, not yet used | Prepaid expense (prepayment) | Current asset | Expense goes **down** |
| Income earned, not yet received | Accrued income | Current asset | Income goes **up** |
| Income received, not yet earned | Income received in advance (deferred income) | Current liability | Income goes **down** |

A quick check: **if you paid in advance, you have an asset** (the right to future service). **If you were paid in advance, you have a liability** (you owe the service).

## Accrued expense — worked example

A business rents a shop for **KES 30,000 a month**. The year ends 31 December. During the year it paid **KES 330,000** (December's rent was paid in January).

- Rent used in the year: 12 × 30,000 = **KES 360,000** → income statement
- Paid: 330,000
- Accrual: 360,000 − 330,000 = **KES 30,000** → current liability

Journal: **Dr** Rent expense 30,000, **Cr** Accrued expenses 30,000

## Prepaid expense — worked example

On 1 October a business pays **KES 120,000** for 12 months' insurance. The year ends 31 December.

- Months used this year: October to December = 3
- Expense: 3/12 × 120,000 = **KES 30,000**
- Prepayment: 9/12 × 120,000 = **KES 90,000** → current asset

Journal: **Dr** Prepayments 90,000, **Cr** Insurance expense 90,000

### "Rent is paid in advance — is that an accrual or a prepayment?"

If **your business pays** rent in advance, it is a **prepayment** (an asset). You have paid for months you have not used yet.

If **your business receives** rent in advance from a tenant, it is **income received in advance** (a liability). You owe the tenant the use of the property.

## Income received in advance — worked example

A landlord receives **KES 15,000** in December for January's rent.

Journal: **Dr** Rent income 15,000, **Cr** Income received in advance 15,000

The KES 15,000 is removed from this year's income and shown as a current liability.

## Next year

Accruals and prepayments reverse at the start of the next period. The KES 90,000 insurance prepayment becomes next year's expense, and when the KES 30,000 accrued rent is paid in January, it clears the liability instead of being charged again.

## Common mistakes

1. Treating cash paid as the expense.
2. Adding a prepayment to the expense instead of subtracting it.
3. Showing an accrual as an asset.
4. Mixing up "we paid in advance" with "we were paid in advance".
