# SBS 201 — Sample CAT 2 with Marking Scheme

> Sample material written for the Arthur demo. Not an official Strathmore University paper.

**Time:** 1 hour. **Total:** 30 marks. Show all workings.

## Question 1 (10 marks)

Jamii Bakers bought an oven on 1 January 2024 for KES 600,000. Delivery and installation cost KES 40,000. The oven has a useful life of 5 years and a residual value of KES 40,000.

a) Calculate the depreciation charge for 2024 using the straight-line method. (3 marks)
b) Calculate the depreciation charges for 2024 and 2025 using the reducing balance method at 30%. (4 marks)
c) Which method better suits an oven? Give a reason. (3 marks)

### Marking scheme

a) Cost = 600,000 + 40,000 = 640,000 (1). Depreciable amount = 640,000 − 40,000 = 600,000 (1). Charge = 600,000 ÷ 5 = **KES 120,000** (1).

b) 2024: 30% × 640,000 = **KES 192,000** (2). 2025: 30% × (640,000 − 192,000) = 30% × 448,000 = **KES 134,400** (2).

c) Accept either method with a reason linked to the pattern of use (IAS 16). For example: straight-line, because an oven used daily gives a similar benefit each year (3). Or reducing balance, because efficiency falls and repair costs rise with age (3). No marks for a method without a reason.

## Question 2 (10 marks)

Tumaini Ltd's year ends 31 December 2024.

- Electricity of KES 18,000 for December 2024 was paid in January 2025. Electricity paid during 2024 was KES 198,000.
- On 1 September 2024, insurance of KES 72,000 was paid for the 12 months to 31 August 2025.

a) Calculate the electricity and insurance expenses for 2024. (6 marks)
b) Show how the year-end balances appear in the statement of financial position. (4 marks)

### Marking scheme

a) Electricity = 198,000 + 18,000 = **KES 216,000** (3). Insurance = 4/12 × 72,000 = **KES 24,000** (3).

b) Accrued electricity KES 18,000 under **current liabilities** (2). Prepaid insurance 8/12 × 72,000 = KES 48,000 under **current assets** (2).

## Question 3 (10 marks)

At 31 March, Upendo Stores' cash book shows a debit balance of KES 56,000 and the bank statement shows a credit balance of KES 61,500. The following items explain the difference:

- Bank charges of KES 800 are not in the cash book.
- A standing order of KES 5,000 for insurance is not in the cash book.
- Cheques totalling KES 19,300 have not been presented.
- A lodgement of KES 8,000 has not been credited by the bank.

a) Prepare the updated cash book balance. (4 marks)
b) Prepare a bank reconciliation statement. (6 marks)

### Marking scheme

a) 56,000 − 800 − 5,000 = **KES 50,200** (4).

b) Balance per bank statement 61,500 (1); add outstanding lodgement 8,000 (2); less unpresented cheques (19,300) (2); balance per updated cash book **KES 50,200** (1).
